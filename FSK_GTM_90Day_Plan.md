## Part A — How PH studios win foreign clients: channel analysis

### What the research says, channel by channel

**Upwork.** Still the fastest path from zero to a paying foreign client, but the economics changed: since May 2025 the freelancer/agency fee is variable 0–15% (typically ~10%, locked per contract), Connects cost $0.15 each, and analysts estimate real all-in agency cost at 22–34% once you count Connects, proposal labor, and withdrawal fees. Winning profiles are narrow specialists ("real estate website developer", "Shopify developer for digital products"), not generalist "full-stack agency" listings. Case studies with measurable outcomes and a small fixed-scope entry offer (e.g., a $1,500 discovery sprint or site audit) outperform hourly generalist bids. Verdict: **use it, but as a wedge for first clients and reviews — not the long-term engine.**

**Toptal.** Individual-vetting model with a hard screen (famously low acceptance); it places individuals rather than studios, so it builds your seniors' resumes more than FSK's brand. **Skip for now.**

**Contra.** Commission-free and growing, good for productized service listings; smaller buyer pool than Upwork. **Secondary — set up a profile in an hour, don't invest beyond that.** (Note: Dixie Raiz Pacheco, the premium PH freelancer in the competitor log, displays a "Contra Best UI Designer Philippines 2026" badge — the platform is visible in the PH-to-global path.)

**Clutch / GoodFirms.** Research is mixed. The pull: 94% of B2B buyers read reviews and Clutch leads are high-intent when they come. The push: agencies widely report the free tier generates credibility but few leads, lead quality complaints are common, and inbound drops to near zero after canceling sponsored listings. Verdict: **free profiles on both, aggressively collect reviews from your first 3–5 clients, don't pay for sponsorship in the first 90 days.**

**Cold outbound (LinkedIn + email).** The best-documented current playbook: micro-segmented lists (one niche, one pain), personalized first lines (2–3x reply lift), value-first assets like a free mini-audit or teardown (≈2.5x more responses than direct pitching), 3–4 email touches plus 1–2 LinkedIn touches. For FSK this is the only channel where you can hand-pick real-estate brokerages and digital-product founders. **Core channel.**

**SEO for English-language buying keywords.** The strongest verified evidence in this research: Qadra, Dixie, Sinag, Praferosa, and OOm all rank with long-form pricing/cost guides, and Dixie — a solo freelancer in Cagayan de Oro — converts foreign clients at $2,500–$8,000 off the back of content that ranks for "web design Philippines" queries. Nobody yet owns "real estate website developer Philippines" with a modern offer. Slow to compound (3–6 months), cheap to start, and FSK already has a Next.js site to publish on. **Core channel, started now so it pays in months 4–9.**

**Referrals / partnerships.** Highest close rate of any channel but requires seed clients; partnership variants (white-label dev for overseas design agencies, fractional/commission-only BD reps who already know CTOs) are proven models for PH studios. **Structural from day one (ask + incentive built into every project close), active pursuit from month 3.**

### The best 2–3 first bets for a studio FSK's size

1. **Niched cold outbound (LinkedIn + email) to two micro-segments** — US/AU independent brokerages & top-producing agents (real-estate line), and founders of digital-product/creator businesses (commerce + app lines). Reasoning: it's the only channel where you choose the client, it validates positioning fastest, costs ~$150/mo in tooling, and every asset it forces you to build (case studies, teardowns, Loom audits) is reusable everywhere else.
2. **Upwork as the proof-and-cashflow wedge.** Reasoning: foreign buyers with budgets are already there; a new studio needs reviews and USD invoices more than it needs margin purity. Accept the ~10% fee + Connects cost as customer-acquisition cost on the first 5–8 projects, filter for $2k+ fixed-scope jobs matching the three service lines, then graduate repeat clients off-platform where rules allow (after the 2-year window or via conversion fee).
3. **SEO/content moat on the three service verticals.** Reasoning: verified to work from the Philippines at premium price points (Dixie, Qadra); FSK's verticals are less contested than generic "web design Philippines"; compounds while the other two channels pay the bills.

Clutch/GoodFirms free profiles are maintained as credibility infrastructure, not counted on for leads. Referral machinery switches on as soon as project #1 ships.

## Part B — The 90-day plan

### Positioning statement

> **FSK Codehouse builds revenue software for property and product businesses — real-estate listing platforms, digital-commerce storefronts, and web/mobile apps — engineered in the Philippines to a global standard.** Fixed prices published, modern stack (Next.js/React Native, not templates), senior engineers on every build, and delivery costs that let overseas clients pay 40–70% less than US/UK agencies — and PH clients get custom engineering at template-shop patience levels. We ship our own products too (Lenso, a private photo-sharing app), so we build like owners, not order-takers.

One-liner: **"PH-engineered. Globally priced. Actually custom."**

### Target client profiles

**PH market**
- *SMB owner-operator* (₱30k–150k budgets): clinics, restaurants, professional services, D2C brands moving off Facebook-only selling. Buys: Starter/Growth sites & storefronts + Care retainer. Found via: FB page, local SEO, referrals, chambers/biz groups.
- *Real-estate agents, teams & boutique brokerages* (₱45k–650k): agents who outgrew Lamudi/OnePropertee dependence and want owned lead capture; brokerages needing listing management. Buys: Agent Starter → Brokerage Growth → Listing Platform. Found via: FB groups for PH real estate practitioners (public posts), broker events, LinkedIn.
- *Funded PH startups / corporates needing a build partner* (₱450k+): MVP or team extension. Found via: founder communities, referrals.

**Global market**
- *US/AU/UK independent brokerage or top-producing agent* ($1.5k–25k): tired of $299/mo SaaS lock-in, wants an owned site/platform. Decision driver: portfolio + price certainty + IDX familiarity.
- *Foreign founder/SME building a product* ($10k–65k): has a validated idea, priced out by $50k+ local quotes. Decision driver: fixed-price MVP, senior team, Lenso as proof FSK ships real products.
- *Overseas design/marketing agency needing white-label dev* ($3k–5.5k/dev/mo): steady team-extension revenue. Decision driver: reliability, timezone overlap (US West Coast evenings / AU mornings align well with PH hours).

### Channel mix & weekly cadence

| Channel | Weekly cadence (steady state, from week 3) |
|---|---|
| Cold outbound | 40 new prospects added to list · 40 personalized first-touches (20/segment) · all follow-ups per sequence (3–4 email + 1–2 LinkedIn touches over 3 weeks) · 3 value assets sent (Loom site teardown or mini-audit) |
| Upwork | 15–25 Connects/week on hand-picked $2k+ fixed-scope jobs (≈5–8 proposals) · profile/portfolio item improved weekly |
| SEO/content | 1 long-form pillar or supporting article per week · 1 case study per shipped project |
| Facebook Page (PH) | 2 posts/week: 1 portfolio/behind-the-scenes, 1 educational (pricing transparency posts mirror the SEO guides) |
| LinkedIn company + founder profile | 3 posts/week (build-in-public: Lenso progress, teardown clips, pricing philosophy) |
| Referrals | Ask at every project close + milestone; 10% referral fee or ₱/$ credit toward Care plans |

Weekly operating rhythm: Mon — list building & content writing; Tue–Thu — outreach blocks (2 hrs/day) + delivery; Fri — proposals, follow-ups, metrics review (30 min: replies, calls booked, proposals out, pipeline value).

### First-client acquisition tactics

**Portfolio & case-study plan (weeks 1–4, before heavy outreach):**
1. Lenso case study — the flagship: problem, architecture, screenshots, App Store link, metrics. Positions FSK as a product studio, not a body shop.
2. Build 2 portfolio-grade demos that double as sales tools: a live **demo brokerage site** (fictional brand, real search/filter/listing CMS) and a live **digital-product storefront** (real checkout in test mode, GCash/Maya + Stripe). Demos beat claims; every cold email links to the relevant one.
3. Land 2 "lighthouse" projects at cost (PH network, real businesses) in exchange for detailed testimonials, Clutch/GoodFirms reviews, and before/after metrics. Deliberately one real-estate, one commerce.

**Platform profile setup checklist:**
- *Upwork (week 1–2):* agency account + founder freelancer profile; specialized profiles per service line; title keyword-matched ("Real Estate Website & Listing Platform Developer — Next.js"); portfolio = the 2 demos + Lenso; intro video under 90 seconds; fixed-scope catalog items mirroring the pricing sheet (Discovery Sprint $1,500 as entry product); proposal template with personalized first line + relevant demo link + one question.
- *Clutch + GoodFirms (week 2):* complete free profiles, all three service lines, PH + "serves US/UK/AU" locations; request reviews from lighthouse clients the day each project ships.
- *LinkedIn (week 1):* company page (banner = positioning one-liner + published prices), founder profile rewritten to sell FSK not employment; both link to demos. Sales Navigator trial from week 3 for list building.
- *Contra (week 2, 1 hour):* mirror of Upwork catalog. 
- *Facebook Page (week 1):* pin a services+prices post (transparent pricing is rare on PH FB — differentiator); enable WhatsApp/Messenger CTAs; keep NAP consistent with Google Business Profile.
- *Google Business Profile + local directories (week 2).*

**Cold outreach templates** (personalize the first line, always reference something real and public):

*Template 1 — US/AU brokerage (value-first):*
> Subject: your listings on [brokerage site] — 3 quick fixes
>

*Template 2 — digital-product founder:*

*Follow-up cadence:* Day 0 email → Day 3 LinkedIn connect + note → Day 6 email (new angle: case study or teardown) → Day 12 email (breakup: "closing the file — one question") → stop. Never more than 4 email touches.

### Milestones

**Day 30**
1. All platform profiles live (Upwork, Clutch, GoodFirms, LinkedIn, Contra, GBP, FB pinned pricing post); pricing published on the FSK site in both bands.
2. Lenso case study + 2 live demos (brokerage, storefront) published.
3. 2 lighthouse projects signed (PH network, at-cost) and in delivery.
4. Outbound engine running: 150+ prospects listed, first 100 touched, ≥10 replies, ≥4 discovery calls held.
5. 4 SEO pillar drafts done, 2 published (start with "Real Estate Website Cost Philippines (2026)" and "How Much Does an MVP Cost? PH vs US Pricing").

**Day 60**
1. First paid foreign engagement closed (any size — a $1.5k Discovery Sprint counts) + 3 Upwork contracts with 5-star outcomes in progress or done.
2. Lighthouse projects shipped; 2 written testimonials + 2 Clutch/GoodFirms reviews live + before/after metrics captured.
3. Pipeline: ≥ $15k / ₱850k in qualified open proposals across both markets.
4. 8 SEO articles live; FSK site ranking-tracked for 10 target keywords; FB page ≥ 2 inbound PH inquiries/week.
5. First white-label partnership conversation reaching proposal stage.

**Day 90**
1. Revenue booked since day 1: ≥ ₱600k–1M equivalent, with ≥ 40% from USD clients (tracks the global-first thesis).
2. 5+ platform reviews (Upwork + Clutch/GoodFirms combined), rating ≥ 4.8.
3. 2 Care retainers + 1 team-extension or white-label agreement signed (recurring base ≥ ₱80k/mo equivalent).
4. One vertical showing repeatable traction (2+ closed deals from the same segment + channel) — this becomes the month-4 doubling-down decision, made from the Friday metrics log.
5. 12 SEO articles + 3 case studies live; at least 1 target keyword on Google page 1–2; outbound reply rate ≥ 8% after template iteration.

### Budget guardrails (first 90 days)
Tools ≈ $120–180/mo (Sales Navigator, email tooling, Loom, Connects); ads ₱0 (organic + outbound only); Clutch sponsorship ₱0; the spend is founder time — roughly 40% delivery, 35% outreach/sales, 25% content.
