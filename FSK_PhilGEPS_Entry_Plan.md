# FSK Codehouse Corp. — PhilGEPS Entry Plan

**Prepared:** 11 August 2026
**Legal basis:** RA 12009 (New Government Procurement Act) and its IRR, 1st Edition as of 30 March 2026

---

## 1. The honest read before you spend anything

Government work is a **real second channel** for FSK, not a replacement for the 90-day GTM plan. Judge it on three things:

**In favour**

- Your three service lines map directly to what LGUs and national agencies actually buy: websites, records and listing systems, e-service portals, and web applications.
- Under RA 12009, **lowest bid no longer automatically wins**. The Most Economically Advantageous Responsive Bid (MEARB) gives quality a legal seat at the table — that favours a competent shop over a cheap one.
- The Small Value Procurement ceiling is **₱2,000,000**, which sits *above* almost your entire published PH price band. Brokerage Growth (₱120–220k), Custom Commerce (₱350–700k) and even MVP Build (₱450–900k) all fit inside SVP. You do not need to win huge contracts for this to be worth it.
- Registration is cheap: **₱5,030/year**.

**Against**

- **Payment is slow.** Government pays after delivery, inspection, and acceptance, and each step has its own queue. Assume months, not weeks. A young company can win a contract and still get into trouble on cash flow.
- **The SLCC requirement blocks you from anything meaningful until you have a track record.** See section 3 — this is the whole game.
- Compliance is recurring admin: annual renewals, sworn statements, updated financials. Someone has to own it.
- Your GTM plan targets USD clients at 2.5–4x the PH band. Government work is PHP, at PH-band margins, with slower collection. It is a diversification play, not a margin play.

**Verdict:** worth doing, in the order below, without letting it eat the outbound and SEO work that pays sooner.

## 2. How the system actually works

**PhilGEPS** is the portal where every government body must post its opportunities. It is a noticeboard and a registry, not a marketplace — you find opportunities there, but you win them by submitting documents to each agency's Bids and Awards Committee (BAC).

Two membership tiers:

| | Red | Platinum |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Free, lifetime | ₱5,030/year (₱5,000 + ₱30 DST) |
| See open and closed opportunities | Yes | Yes |
| See award notices and bid abstracts | Yes | Yes |
| Download bid documents | No | Yes |
| Submit bids | No | Yes |
| Certificate of Registration (what BACs ask for) | No | Yes |

You must hold Red before you can upgrade. Platinum approval takes around **7 business days** once documents are accepted.

**Award notices are the single most useful free thing on the platform.** They tell you who won, for how much, and against what budget. Read them before you bid on anything.

### The three procurement categories

Everything is classified as **Goods**, **Infrastructure**, or **Consulting Services**. Mixed projects are classified by their *primary purpose*.

This matters to you because software lands in different buckets depending on how the agency writes it up:

- "Development of a website / information system" with a defined deliverable → usually **Goods** (non-expendable) or Consulting, depending on the agency
- "IT consultancy, systems study, requirements analysis" → **Consulting Services**
- Software bundled into a building or network rollout → **Infrastructure**

The category determines which eligibility rules and bidding documents apply. Check it on every opportunity — do not assume.

## 3. The barrier that decides everything: SLCC

For competitive bidding, the **Single Largest Completed Contract** rule (IRR Sec. 52.4.1.3) requires the bidder to have:

- **one** completed contract *similar to the project* worth at least **50% of the ABC**, adjusted to current prices using the PSA Consumer Price Index; **or**
- **up to three** completed contracts totalling at least **50% of the ABC**, where the largest single one is at least **25% of the ABC**

Completed within **five years** before the bid deadline. For expendable goods the threshold is 25% instead of 50%.

**What this means concretely.** On a ₱2,000,000 project you must show ₱1,000,000 of similar completed work. On a ₱10,000,000 project, ₱5,000,000.

There is an SLCC exemption for newly established firms bidding infrastructure within their PCAB category. **It does not help you** — software is not PCAB-registered infrastructure.

### The one question that determines your whole strategy

**Do FSK's private-sector contracts count toward SLCC?**

In general practice, "similar contract" refers to the *nature of the work*, not the type of client, and private contracts are commonly accepted when supported by the contract, a certificate of completion or acceptance from the client, and proof of payment. **But I could not verify a definitive GPPB ruling on this, and interpretation varies between BACs.**

Confirm this before anything else — see section 8. If private contracts count, your existing client work becomes your entry ticket and you can bid meaningfully in year one. If they don't, you must build a government track record from the bottom, and the plan below is the only route.

### Three ways past it

1. **Start below the threshold.** Small Value Procurement (≤₱2M) uses Request for Quotation, not full competitive bidding. In practice SVP RFQs typically ask only for your PhilGEPS registration number, Mayor's permit, BIR registration, and a notarised Omnibus Sworn Statement — not SLCC. *Confirm per RFQ, since the procuring entity sets the requirements.*

2. **Subcontract to an existing government supplier.** Fastest way to revenue and relationships. Caution: the certificate of completion normally goes to the prime contractor, so this may build capability and cash without building *your* SLCC. Ask upfront whether they will issue a certificate naming FSK for your scope.

3. **Joint venture with an eligible firm.** A JV lets you borrow a partner's track record. More paperwork, and you share the margin — but it is a legitimate path onto larger contracts while you build your own history.

## 4. Phase 1 — Register (weeks 1–4)

### Step 1: Red membership (free, ~1 day)

Register the corporation at **philgeps.gov.ph**. You need the SEC registration and TIN. Do this now regardless of whether you pursue Platinum — the award-notice data alone is worth it.

### Step 2: Gather the Platinum documents

Prepare digital copies of:

- [ ] SEC Certificate of Registration, Articles of Incorporation, and By-laws
- [ ] Latest General Information Sheet (GIS) filed with SEC
- [ ] Valid Mayor's / Business Permit for the current year
- [ ] BIR Certificate of Registration (Form 2303)
- [ ] Latest Audited Financial Statements, stamped received by BIR
- [ ] Latest Income Tax Return
- [ ] Tax Clearance from BIR
- [ ] Notarised Omnibus Sworn Statement
- [ ] Secretary's Certificate or Board Resolution naming your authorised representative
- [ ] Valid government ID of that representative

> **Watch this one.** If FSK Codehouse Corp. is newly incorporated and has not yet filed a full-year Audited Financial Statement, Platinum registration may stall. Sort the AFS and Tax Clearance first — they take the longest and everything else waits on them.

### Step 3: Upgrade to Platinum

₱5,030. Roughly 7 business days after documents are accepted. Diarise the **annual renewal now** — a lapsed registration disqualifies you mid-bid, and it happens to people constantly.

## 5. Phase 2 — Learn the market before bidding (weeks 2–8)

Do this in parallel with registration. **Do not bid on anything yet.**

**Set up monitoring.** On PhilGEPS, filter opportunities by category and keyword: *website, web application, information system, software development, database, portal, digitization, records management, management system*. Check twice a week.

**Study 20 award notices** for IT projects in the last 12 months. For each, record:

- Procuring entity, and whether LGU or national agency
- ABC vs the winning bid
- Who won, and whether the same names keep appearing
- How the requirement was written up, and which category it was filed under

After twenty of these you will know your realistic price points, who you are actually competing against, and which agencies buy the kind of work you do. This is the highest-value unpaid work in the whole plan.

**Pick two or three target agencies.** Best odds for FSK:

- **LGUs** — city and municipal governments needing websites, business permit systems, records digitisation. Smaller budgets, less competition, easier to reach a human.
- **State universities and colleges** — student portals, records systems, enrolment tools.
- **Regional offices of national agencies** — DICT, DTI, DOT, DA. They procure separately from head office and at smaller scale.

Avoid national head-office IT procurement in year one. The ABCs look attractive and the incumbents are entrenched.

## 6. Phase 3 — First contracts (months 2–9)

**Target: two completed government contracts, any size.** Not profit. Track record.

**Register as a known supplier.** Under SVP, the BAC sends RFQs to "at least three suppliers of known qualifications" — meaning they pick from suppliers they know exist. Introduce FSK to the BAC secretariat of your target agencies with a one-page capability profile: services, PhilGEPS registration number, sample work, contact. This is legitimate and normal. Do it before an RFQ exists, not after.

**Watch for SVP opportunities under ₱2M.** For LGUs the ceiling is lower and depends on classification — reported to range from around ₱1,000,000 for first-class provinces and cities down to ₱50,000 for barangays. Confirm the applicable threshold with each LGU.

**Bid seriously on the small ones.** A ₱300,000 LGU website is not exciting revenue. It is your SLCC for a ₱600,000 bid next year, and your ₱600,000 contract is your SLCC for ₱1.2M. That compounding is the entire reason to do this.

**Deliver visibly and get the paperwork.** On completion, obtain a **Certificate of Completion / Certificate of Satisfactory Performance** naming FSK, plus the contract and proof of payment. Without these documents the contract does not exist for eligibility purposes. Chase them while people still remember you.

---

## 7. Phase 4 — Scale (month 9 onward)

- Move up one bracket at a time. Your SLCC caps you at roughly 2x your largest completed contract.
- Keep a live eligibility folder so a bid submission is assembly, not scrambling. Most losses at this stage are administrative, not competitive.
- With MEARB in force, invest in the technical proposal. Quality now carries legal weight — write real methodology, staffing, and delivery plans rather than pricing to the floor.
- Once you have three or four completed government contracts, consider JV-ing as the *technical* partner on larger builds.

## 8. Confirm these before committing

Ambiguity here changes the plan, so get answers in writing.

1. **Do private-sector contracts count toward SLCC for Goods and Consulting?** Ask the **GPPB Technical Support Office** (gppb.gov.ph) and get the reply in writing. This is the single highest-value question in this document.
2. **What exactly does each target agency require for SVP?** Requirements vary by procuring entity. Get a sample RFQ from two target LGUs.
3. **How is your kind of work classified** — Goods or Consulting? It changes the eligibility documents and the bidding forms.
4. **Bid security and performance security** — these exist and are usually a percentage of the ABC or contract price, in cash, surety, or bank guarantee. Confirm the current rates and acceptable forms under the RA 12009 IRR before you budget for a bid. Do not rely on figures from the old RA 9184 era.
5. **Actual payment timelines** at your target agencies. Ask an existing supplier, not the agency.

## 10. Risks

**Cash flow is the one that kills companies.** You deliver, then wait through inspection, acceptance, and processing before payment. Do not accept a government contract you cannot fund to completion out of other revenue. Two contracts running at once with delayed payment on both is a genuinely dangerous position for a company your size.

**Compliance lapses disqualify you silently.** An expired Mayor's permit or lapsed PhilGEPS registration ends a bid regardless of how good the proposal is.

**Opportunity cost.** Time on procurement paperwork is time not spent on outbound, which pays sooner. If the two conflict in the first 90 days, outbound wins.

**Integrity.** You will eventually meet someone suggesting an arrangement. Refuse, in writing where possible, and walk away from that agency. A blacklisting ends the channel permanently and follows the company.

## 11. Do this first

1. Register for **Red membership** this week. Free, and the award notices start teaching you immediately.
2. Email the **GPPB-TSO** the SLCC question in section 8.1.
3. Check whether FSK has a **filed AFS and Tax Clearance**. If not, start there — it gates Platinum.
4. Read **20 IT award notices** and write down what you learn.
5. Decide after those four steps whether to spend the ₱5,030. Nothing above commits you until then.
