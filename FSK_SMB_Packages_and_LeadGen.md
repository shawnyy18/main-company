## 1. Should you build a CRM? No — not yet

Straight answer: **don't build it.** Here's why, and what to build instead.

**You have zero leads from this channel.** A CRM manages a problem you don't have yet. Building one now is weeks of engineering time spent on infrastructure instead of on the thing that actually produces revenue, which is talking to business owners.

**A spreadsheet handles your first 300 leads without complaint.** Google Sheets is free, works today, and every column you need is in §6. When it genuinely breaks — and you'll know, because you'll be losing follow-ups — pay for HubSpot's free tier or use Notion. Building your own CRM is a decision for a company with 20 salespeople, not one closing its first client.

**The bottleneck isn't organising leads. It's finding and contacting them.**

### On "messaging them through the platform"

Read this part carefully, because it can cost you your Facebook page.

**Automating Facebook Messenger DMs violates Meta's platform terms.** Pages that bulk-message get restricted or removed. Your GTM plan puts the FB page at the centre of PH lead generation — risking it to save a few hours of manual work is a bad trade. Manual DMs, sent by a human, are completely fine.

**Cold email to published business addresses is the defensible channel.** Under the Data Privacy Act, business contact information — work email, business phone, job title — is excluded from the definition of personal data when used for B2B purposes. Emailing a clinic's published `info@` address about web development does not require DPA consent.

**What is not safe:** scraping personal Facebook profiles, harvesting personal mobile numbers, or taking contact details collected for one purpose and reusing them for marketing. The NPC's position is that repurposing contact data for marketing needs fresh consent, and administrative fines run from 0.5% to 3% of annual gross income, capped at ₱5M per violation.

Rule of thumb: **a business's published contact details, used to contact that business, is fine. A person's details are not.**

### What to build instead (a few hours, not weeks)

A **prospect checker**. Give it a list of business names and locations; it tells you which ones have no website, or a broken/non-mobile one. That's your actual bottleneck, it's a genuinely small script, and it turns a day of manual checking into ten minutes.

That's the whole tool. Resist making it more.

## 2. Who to target

There is no reliable published figure for what share of Philippine MSMEs have a website — I looked, and the recent data doesn't exist in a form worth quoting. Don't plan around a number someone made up. Measure your own city instead; §5 shows how, and it takes an afternoon.

What we do know: MSME e-commerce and digital adoption in the Philippines has historically been low, and DTI is actively pushing MSMEs to digitise. The gap is real. Size it yourself.

### Priority sectors

Ranked by how easy they are to reach and how obvious the pain is.

| Sector | Why they're a good target | What they buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Dental & medical clinics** | Patients search before booking. Most run on a FB page with no hours, no services, no booking. High-value customers, so a website pays for itself fast. | Business Website — Professional |
| **Real estate agents & brokers** | Your existing vertical. Leads currently go to Lamudi and OnePropertee instead of them. | Agent Starter / Brokerage Growth |
| **Law, accounting, architecture firms** | Credibility-driven. A FB page looks unserious next to a competitor with a real site. | Business Website — Professional |
| **Veterinary clinics, diagnostic labs** | Same dynamic as clinics, less competition for your attention | Business Website — Starter/Professional |
| **Restaurants & cafés with delivery** | Paying platform commissions; a direct ordering page saves them money | Online Store — Starter |
| **Schools, review centres, training providers** | Enrolment is seasonal and online; parents search | Business Website — Complete |

**Start with clinics.** The pain is easiest to demonstrate, the buyer is one person, and the decision doesn't need a committee.

## 5. Finding businesses without websites

I'm not going to hand you a made-up list of real business names — you'd waste days chasing bad data. Here's the method to build a real one in an afternoon.

### The fastest source: Google Maps

Search `dental clinic [your city]`. For each result, the listing either shows a website link or it doesn't. **No website link = a qualified lead**, with the business name, address, and phone already on screen.

Work one barangay or one city at a time. Aim for 50 businesses in your first session.

### Secondary sources

- **Facebook page search** — `dental clinic [city]`. Check the About tab for a website. Many have none, or link to a dead Wix page.
- **DTI business name registry** — newly registered businesses that almost certainly have nothing yet.
- **Local business groups and chambers** — member directories are lists of real businesses with contact details.
- **Your own city's permit listings**, where published.

### Qualify each one

| Qualifies | Skip |
|---|---|
| No website at all | Already has a good, recent, mobile-friendly site |
| Site is broken or won't load | Franchise or chain (decision made at head office) |
| Not mobile-friendly | Business looks dormant |
| Clearly abandoned — old copyright year, dead links | No contact details anywhere |
| Free Wix/Weebly subdomain | |

**Target: 100 qualified businesses before you send anything.** Working from a real list beats messaging as you go.

## 7. The outreach system

### Daily: 10 messages

Not 50. Ten personalised messages beat fifty copy-pastes, and ten is sustainable alongside delivery work.

### The hook that works for no-website businesses

Before messaging, spend 60 seconds: **Google their business name and screenshot the result.** For a clinic with no website, the result is a bare Maps pin or a Facebook page — and that screenshot is your entire pitch. It's specific, it's about them, and it costs you nothing.

**Do not build them a free sample site.** That's spec work. You give away the thing you're selling and signal your time is worthless.

### Message template — clinic, FB page

> Hi Doc! Nag-search po ako ng "[clinic name]" sa Google at ito lang po ang lumalabas — [screenshot].
>
> Ibig sabihin po, pag may naghahanap ng dentist sa [city], mahirap kayong makita — kahit malapit lang sila sa clinic niyo.
>
> Kami po ang FSK Codehouse, gumagawa kami ng website para sa clinics: services, schedule, location, at appointment form na diretso sa staff niyo. Starts at ₱45,000, live in 2–3 weeks.
>
> May sample po kami dito: [link]
>
> Pwede po ba kayong ma-15 minutes this week?

### Message template — email, professional firm

> Subject: [Firm name] — quick note about your website
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I searched for [firm name] and couldn't find a website — just a Facebook page.
>
> For firms like yours, clients almost always check online before calling. Right now they're finding your competitors instead.
>
> We build professional firm websites from ₱45,000, live in 2–3 weeks. Published fixed prices, no hourly billing.
>
> Recent work: fskcodehouse.com
>
> Worth 15 minutes this week?
>
> Shawn — FSK Codehouse Corp.

### Follow-up — where most sales are actually won

- **Day 3:** "Hi [Name], just following up on my message."
- **Day 10:** Send something useful — a competitor's site, a relevant article from your blog.
- **Day 21:** "Should I close your file for now?" — this one gets more replies than anything else.

Three follow-ups. Then stop and move on.

## 8. Expect these numbers

Ratios for cold outreach to small businesses, as a planning baseline — track your own and replace these once you have real data.

| | |
|---|---|
| Messages sent | 200/month (10/day × 20 working days) |
| Replies | 10–20 |
| Calls booked | 3–6 |
| Closed | 1–2 |

**One or two clients a month at ₱45,000–85,000.** That's ₱45k–170k monthly from a channel that costs you nothing but time, plus Care plan revenue that stacks month over month.

If you're getting under 5 replies per 200 messages, the problem is the message, not the volume. Rewrite the hook before sending more.
