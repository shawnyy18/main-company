# Pricing Review — Business & Real-Estate Package Menus

**Reviewed:** 29 August 2026
**Against:** `FSK_Pricing_PH_Global.xlsx` · `FSK_PH_Competitor_Research.xlsx` · `FSK_SMB_Packages_and_LeadGen.md` · live market check
**Verdict:** structure is good, terms are above average for the PH market, and the entry price is the one thing that needs to change before you send these out.

---

## 1. What's working

Worth saying first, because most of this is right.

- **The ladder shape is correct.** Four tiers, each with a plain "best for" line, ending in a discovery-gated custom quote. That is exactly how a studio should present work.
- **The honesty is a competitive advantage.** "Properties are manually displayed. This package does not include a listing-management dashboard." and "FSK uploads the initial listings" prevent the two most common disputes in real-estate builds. Almost nobody states this upfront.
- **Terms are more complete than most PH shops publish.** 50/50 split, 14-day validity, timeline starting after deposit *and* content, client-supplied assets, exclusions for hosting and third-party subscriptions — all correct and all load-bearing.
- **Real-estate laddering is well judged.** ₱34,999 → ₱89,999 → custom maps cleanly to solo agent → small brokerage → developer. The feature deltas justify the jumps.

## 2. The one serious problem: ₱8,999 is below the market floor

Not "cheap." Below the floor.

| Source | One-page / entry site in PH |
| --- | --- |
| [Oro Digital](https://orodigital.ph/blog/website-cost-philippines/) | ₱10,000 – ₱35,000 |
| [Praferosa Webworks](https://www.praferosawebworks.com/blog/website-cost-philippines-2026/) | from ~₱18,000 for a clean one-page business site |
| [NetizenWorks](https://netizenworks.com/pricing/) (budget template shop) | ₱14,370 + ₱980–1,200/mo |
| Your own competitor research | budget shops ₱8k–14k; typical basic site ₱15k–40k |
| **Your Launch Page** | **₱8,999** |

You have priced your entry tier at the absolute bottom of the budget template-shop band — under NetizenWorks, whose whole business model is volume WordPress templates with a mandatory hosting subscription.

**Three consequences:**

**It contradicts your own positioning.** `FSK_Pricing_PH_Global.xlsx` states the PH band is "positioned in the genuine custom gap — above template shops." Your competitor research identifies Gap #3 as "the middle band (₱75k–150k genuine custom work) is underserved." You found an underserved gap, then priced yourself into the most crowded, most price-competitive segment in the market.

**It anchors every other number.** ₱8,999 is the first figure a prospect reads. Against that anchor, ₱89,999 reads as 10x, not as "a brokerage system." Buyers do not evaluate tiers independently — they evaluate the *ratio*.

**The margin is a junior rate.** Realistic delivery for Launch Page, assuming a responsive client:

| Activity | Hours |
| --- | --- |
| Kickoff call and requirements | 1.0 |
| Chasing and formatting client content | 1.5 |
| Design and build, five sections | 7.0 |
| Revision round | 2.0 |
| Deployment, QA, mobile checks | 1.5 |
| Handover and post-launch fixes | 1.5 |
| **Total** | **~14.5** |

₱8,999 ÷ 14.5 h ≈ **₱620/hour** — before lead acquisition, payment fees, admin, and tax. Your own rate table puts junior PH developers at $10–20/hr (₱580–1,160). You would be selling senior end-to-end delivery at the bottom of the junior band.

And the hours above assume a *responsive* client. The ₱8,999 buyer is typically the least digitally mature, needs the most hand-holding, and supplies content slowest. This tier does not make money. It makes work.

### Two ways to fix it

**Option A — raise to the floor.** Launch Page becomes **₱14,999–18,999**. You sit at market rate rather than under it, the ratio to ₱24,999 tightens sensibly, and the anchor stops damaging the top of the menu.

**Option B — keep ₱8,999, require a care plan.** ₱8,999 build + mandatory 12-month care at ₱1,500/mo = **₱26,999 contract value**, with ₱18,000 of it recurring. This is precisely the NetizenWorks model, and it converts your weakest tier into your best one, because recurring revenue is worth more than project revenue.

**Option B is the stronger play** — it keeps an aggressive headline number for lead generation while fixing the economics underneath. It only works if you actually build the care plan, which brings us to the bigger omission.

## 3. The biggest miss: there are no recurring revenue lines

Neither menu prices maintenance. "Additional listing updates can be covered by a maintenance plan" appears once, under Agent Portfolio, and is never defined or priced.

You already did this research. `FSK_Pricing_PH_Global.xlsx` has a fully costed three-tier plan (Care Basic ₱4–8k/mo, Care Plus ₱12–22k/mo, Care Pro ₱25–50k/mo), and your competitor research flags Gap #7: *"Maintenance retainers are either bundled-cheap or premium-vague. A clean 3-tier care plan is an opportunity."* You identified the opening and then left it out of the menu.

This matters more than the entry price. Project revenue is lumpy and every month starts at zero. Ten clients on ₱4,000/month is ₱480,000/year that arrives whether or not you close anything new. It is the difference between a studio and a series of gigs.

**Add a third menu.** Three tiers, one page, attached to every proposal as the default rather than an upsell:

| | Care Basic | Care Plus | Care Pro |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly | ₱2,500 | ₱7,500 | ₱18,000 |
| Hosting, SSL, backups, monitoring | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Security patches and bug fixes | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Content or listing updates | — | 2 hrs/mo | 8 hrs/mo |
| Monthly report | — | ✓ | ✓ |
| Response time | 3 business days | 1 business day | Same business day |

These sit below your xlsx figures deliberately — those were set against agency-grade benchmarks, and the clients buying ₱8,999–34,999 builds will not pay ₱12,000/month. Priced at ₱2,500 the Basic tier is an easy yes at handover, which is the only moment a client ever agrees to a subscription.

**Also missing:** hosting and domain are listed as client-paid exclusions. That is revenue you are handing away. Reselling managed hosting inside a care plan is standard, and it is why NetizenWorks bundles it.

## 4. Internal inconsistencies to resolve

**Four documents, four different prices for the same work.**

| Document | Entry real-estate site |
| --- | --- |
| `FSK_Codehouse_Pricing_Structure.xlsx` (22 Jul) | ₱55,575 |
| `FSK_Pricing_PH_Global.xlsx` (24 Jul) | ₱45,000 |
| `FSK_SMB_Packages_and_LeadGen.md` (11 Aug) | ₱45,000 |
| **New menu (29 Aug)** | **₱8,999** |

If a prospect ever sees two of these, or if you quote from memory on a call, you have a credibility problem. Pick one source of truth and mark the others superseded.

**Gaps invite haggling.** ₱8,999 → ₱24,999 is 2.8x. ₱8,999 → ₱34,999 is 3.9x. Jumps that large produce "can I get something in between?" on every call. Fixing the entry price largely fixes this.

**The Business ladder now tops out too low.** Business Websites run ₱8,999 → ₱24,999 → ₱49,999 → custom. Nothing sits in the ₱75k–150k band your research calls underserved. Real Estate covers it at ₱89,999; Business does not. Either add a fourth tier around ₱89,999 or accept that all mid-market business work goes to private quotation.

**Your best case study does not fit your own menu.** Marketlink is 114 products across 6 categories, with filtering, per-product inquiry, and a wholesale section. A prospect reading this menu would ask for "Growth Website, ₱49,999." That is not what that build was. You need a stated boundary — something like *"catalogues above 40 items, or any site with search and filtering, are quoted individually"* — or the menu will underprice the exact work you are proudest of.

## 5. Terms: six clauses to add

The terms are good. These six gaps are the ones that cost money.

1. **Define a revision round.** Currently unbounded. Use: *"A revision round is one consolidated set of written feedback, submitted within 5 business days. Feedback sent piecemeal or after that window is a new round."* Without this, "two revision rounds" becomes two months.

2. **Put a number on the bug-fix period.** "Every project includes a post-launch bug-fix period" is unenforceable in both directions. Use 30 days, and define a bug as *"something that does not work as specified"* — distinct from a change of mind.

3. **Add a client-delay clause.** Timelines start after deposit and complete content, but nothing covers content that never arrives. Use: *"If materials are outstanding for more than 30 days, the project is paused; restarting is subject to availability and may incur a reactivation fee."* Otherwise a ₱8,999 project sits open for eight months.

4. **State IP transfer.** Nothing says who owns the site. Standard: *"Ownership of the delivered site transfers on final payment. FSK retains the right to display the work in its portfolio."* You need the second half for `/work` — it is what makes the Marketlink case study safe.

5. **Add late payment and cancellation terms.** What happens if the final 50% does not arrive? What if the client cancels mid-build? Suggested: work completed to date is billable, and the deposit is non-refundable once design work has started.

6. **Say something about tax.** Prices are stated bare. Add *"Prices are exclusive of any applicable taxes"* at minimum. Separately — and worth an accountant's fifteen minutes — DTI registration is not BIR registration. Issuing official receipts is a separate registration, and clients who need a receipt for their own books will ask. Confirm where you stand before invoicing.

## 6. One strategic contradiction worth deciding

Your terms say *"Prices are shared privately and are not intended for public posts."*

Your competitor research says the opposite, as Gap #5: *"Content-SEO moat around pricing keywords is the visible acquisition engine. Qadra, Dixie, Sinag, Praferosa, and OOm all rank via long-form 'web design Philippines pricing' guides."*

Every competitor winning search traffic in this market does it by publishing prices. Your new website currently shows none at all.

These are reconcilable: **publish "starts at" figures publicly, keep the detailed menus private.** A pricing page saying "Business websites from ₱X. Real-estate sites from ₱Y. Every project is quoted after a discovery call." captures the search intent, filters out people with no budget before they reach your inbox, and gives you something to rank for — without publishing the full menu.
